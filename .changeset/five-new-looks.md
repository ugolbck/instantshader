Four new looks: `silk` (satin folds with an anisotropic sheen; matte and grainy at `sheen: 0`), `nacre` (glassy liquid folds whose colour shifts through the palette on every slope), `burst` (thin grainy rays out of one point, from dark tunnel to white-hot star, straight or twisted) and `glint` (a shoal of spinning shards streaming along a current). React: `<Silk>`, `<Nacre>`, `<Burst>`, `<Glint>`.

Halftone now draws over the picture instead of replacing it. New params: `ground` (`image`, the default, keeps the picture visible between the shapes; `paper` is the old shapes-on-a-sheet look), `blend` (`normal`, `multiply`, `screen`, `overlay`, `softLight`, `colorDodge`, the CSS blend modes), `opacity`, `blur` (blurs the picture behind the shapes, image ground only), `style` (`filled` sizes shapes by tone, `uniform` makes them all one size), `exposure`, `density` (the share of cells that get a shape) and `contrast`. On an image ground, multiplied shapes grow in the darks and every other blend grows them in the brights; on paper they grow away from the paper colour. `invert` flips it. New defaults: screen, diamonds (`shape: square` at 45°), size 20, radius 0.75, softness 0.1 (was 0), contrast 1.15 (was 1), source colours (was duotone). For the previous look, pass `{ ground: "paper", blend: "normal", shape: "dot", size: 28, radius: 1, softness: 0, contrast: 1, colorMode: "duotone" }`. `pulse` is removed. In palette mode, halftone and ASCII now colour each dot or glyph with the nearest palette stop, flat; over a shader they used to hand back the source colour and looked identical to source mode.
